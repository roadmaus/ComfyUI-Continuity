Why the bench needs a redo

  1. Everything lives in one scrolling rail. Set, presets, snap, camera, lens, frame, path, output and the file path are nine stops in one column, and most hints are cut off mid-sentence.
  2. There are two cameras to manage. Free look, Through the lens, Put the camera here, Go to the camera: you work a camera to look at the camera.
  3. Blocking is done with 3D handles. Moving a box on the floor through a perspective view is the fiddliest way to do the one thing a director sketches flat on paper.
  4. The move is invisible. Marks are text rows (Mark 1, 0:00.0) and the scrub bar is empty. You cannot see the frames you marked.
  5. The output is chosen twice. The pass list sits at the bottom of the rail and a Stage/Depth switch sits on the glass.
  6. The best output is the smallest thing on screen. The staging sentence, the part you paste into the prompt, is a grey italic line at the foot.

What all three take from the reel

One surfaceNo rail of stops. The work is in the middle and one inspector shows whatever is selected, or the shot when nothing is.
The move at its real lengthThe marks sit on a ruler to scale, the shot's end is dragged to change its length, and A shows each mark's frame.
Undo and one buttonCtrl+Z takes back any change. The header says what the render will write, and one button writes it.
The words up frontThe staging sentence is the caption under the lens in A and B, and the whole editor in C.

Recommendation

Build B as the bench, with C's sentence across its top as the way in, and A's frame thumbnails on its ruler. Most people start by saying the shot in C, get a working set and move, then drag things on the plan where the words run out. The lens stays one camera, shown as a monitor. These three tabs already share one scene, so that path is how this page behaves today.
