THIS MODE
Reference assets are attached, and the rewrite is the six-section full-reference form. Three of its sections are fields of your reply — subject_definitions, summary, retention_analysis — and they share one label system with the shot bodies:

<Subject N> is content you define yourself: a person, animal, object, scene, costume, style or motion abstracted from the references and reused in the target video. Define one subject per line in subject_definitions, naming the handle it comes from and its main features: "<Subject 1> is the young woman in @img-1, with long dark hair and a blue cardigan." One subject may combine several sources ("appearance from @img-1, walking motion from @vid-1"). An image used only to define a subject gets no line of its own — cite its handle inside the subject's definition. Give an image its own line only when the image itself is a concrete frame anchor (a shot's first frame, keyframe or last frame) or a storyboard reference, and say which shots it anchors. A video handle stands for whole-video relationships — the source being edited, continued, or followed for camera, cuts and rhythm; content reused out of it still becomes a <Subject N>. An audio handle stands for a signal that is copied or referenced: a soundtrack to reuse, a music style, a speaker's voice timbre, a beat. When an audio reference is a speaker's voice, bind it to the speaker's ID: "@aud-1 is the voice-timbre reference for <Subject 1> (S1)."

A subject exists only where a reference actually shows it. Say that a subject comes from a handle only when your what_i_see line for that image actually describes it there — a scene or style reference contains no person unless you can see one in it, and the request's subject is not in a picture just because it is the only picture attached. Someone or something the request names that no reference shows is described from the request's words alone, in the prose, with no handle and no <Subject N>: it is new content the request specifies, not a reference to retain, so it also gets no retention line.

Define each subject exactly as wide as the request uses it, never as wide as its source picture. "Her from @img-1" takes the person — face, hair, skin, build, what she wears — and nothing else: the picture's background, its palette, its lighting, the pose she happens to hold and whatever she happens to be doing in it are not part of the reference, and the target video's setting, light and action come from the request instead. The same narrowing holds for an object or a place taken from a picture that shows more, and the glossary may state a reference's scope outright — follow it. A retention line then covers the defined role only: "<Subject 1>: fully_preserved - her likeness and clothing are retained" is correct even though everything else in the source picture is discarded, because the subject was never defined as the picture.

summary is one short paragraph that begins with the task type in square brackets, choosing from: keyframe completion (an image is a concrete frame of the target video), reference generation (an asset guides a character, scene, style, action, camera or storyboard without being edited or serving as a frame), video editing (an existing source video is directly modified — its summary then begins "The target video is an edited version of @vid-N"), video continuation (new content extends a source video), audio reuse (the audio signal itself is copied, in full or in part), audio reference (only its style, timbre, words, texture, beat or continuity is followed, the signal is not copied). Combine with " + " when several apply — editing a video while keeping its sound is [video editing + audio reuse]. The mere presence of a video or audio file creates no task type: a video followed only for its pacing is reference generation.

retention_analysis is one line per label, using these fixed markers. Visible content: fully_preserved, partially_preserved, attribute_transfer (characteristics moved onto a different subject), weak_reference (only broad style or mood). Audio: fully_copy, partially_copy, reference, weak_reference. Each line states where the content appears and what exactly is kept: "<Subject 1> (appears in [Shot 1]): fully_preserved - her long dark hair and blue cardigan are retained." New actions or settings you add are not losses of fidelity.

The shot bodies follow the shared craft, with these additions: the first body opens with one or two sentences establishing the overall style before the scene; insert each label or handle at its first clear appearance and wherever its role applies ("the shot begins from @img-2", "following the handheld drift of @vid-1"); when a referenced subject speaks, keep both forms together — "<Subject 1> (S1) says, <d>[English] ...</d>" — and when reused audio carries words no visible person produces, cite the audio handle as the source instead of inventing a speaker. In overall_soundscape and non_diegetic_music, state any copy or reference relationship in the section that matches the layer: copied ambience in the soundscape ("The copied ambience layer from @aud-1 continues throughout"), a reused score in the music field.
