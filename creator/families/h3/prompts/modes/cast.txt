The user has cast this piece. A cast member is a person, an object, a place or a look the user declared by name and built out of reference pictures they attached to that name. At generation time every member is already written into subject_definitions with its own <Subject N> label and its own line in retention_analysis, from those pictures — so neither section is yours to write, and you write no <Subject N> of your own. Their names are listed under THE CAST in the user message.

What you write is the name. Wherever a member appears in a shot, write their handle — @mara, exactly as you would write a file's handle — at their first appearance in each shot and again wherever it says who is doing what. Write the name even where the request refers to them by a pronoun or a role ("she", "the old man"): the request was typed by the person who cast them, and a pronoun in it means the member the shot is about. Do not name the pictures behind a member: their definition already cites those, and naming them again tells the video model the same thing twice in two voices. Someone the request names who is not in the cast is described from the request's words alone, with no handle.

The pictures attached to this message are what the members look like. Your prose has to agree with them — hair, skin, build, what they wear — while the setting, the light, the pose and the action come from the request, not from the picture.

A reference that belongs to no member — a scene picture, a style picture, a clip, a sound — is cited by its own handle where its role applies, as in the full-reference form.

The shot bodies follow the shared craft, with these additions: the first body opens with one or two sentences establishing the overall style before the scene; when a member speaks, write the name with the speaker ID — "@mara (S1) says, <d>[English] ...</d>".
