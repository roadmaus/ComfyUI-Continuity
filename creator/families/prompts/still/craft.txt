HOW A STILL IS WRITTEN

One flowing paragraph of plain prose. No headers, no bullet points, no labels
such as "Subject:", "Style:" or "Lighting:", and no keyword lists strung
together with commas. Every image model this node drives reads a sentence
better than a tag pile, and a field name standing where a description belongs
is noise the model has to draw around.

This is one picture, not a video. Nothing moves, nothing happens next, and
there is no sound. Where the request describes an action, write the single
instant of it that the picture holds — the ball just leaving the hand, the
door half open — and describe it as held there. Never write camera movement,
a sequence of events, "then", "begins to", dialogue or audio.

Say it in this order, and let it flow rather than listing it:

THE FRAME
Open with what kind of picture it is and how it is framed: a photograph, an
oil painting, a flat vector illustration, a 3D render, a film still; a wide
establishing view, a medium portrait, a tight close-up, an overhead flat lay,
a low angle. Name a medium or a style where the request implies one and then
describe the look it actually has — the brushwork, the grain, the line
quality, the palette — rather than trusting the name to carry it.

THE SUBJECT
Who or what the picture is of, where it sits in the frame, and what it looks
like: age, build, hair, clothing, materials, colours, distinguishing marks, the
pose and the expression held. Emotion is a physical cue — a tightened jaw,
dropped shoulders — not an abstract label. A few clear subjects read better
than a crowded scene.

THE SETTING
Where it is, what is behind and around the subject, and how far away the
background sits.

THE LIGHT
One coherent light logic: where the light comes from, its quality and colour,
what it catches and what falls into shadow. Then the overall palette and the
atmosphere — haze, dust, rain on glass.

THE CAMERA
Where it fits the medium: lens length, depth of field, focus, film stock or
sensor character, exposure. A request that names equipment is asking for the
picture that equipment makes, so describe that picture.

TEXT IN THE PICTURE
Words that should appear in the image — a sign, a label, a title — go in
double quotation marks, exactly as the request gives them, with where they sit
and how they are lettered: `a hand-painted sign reading "OPEN LATE" above the
door`. Keep lettering short and prominent.

LENGTH
Roughly four to eight sentences. A close-up wants more surface detail than a
wide view; an illustration wants more about its style than a photograph does.
