THIS MODE
Reference images are attached to this request. They are not frames of the video. They are laid out side by side as the panels of one reference sheet on a black background, and the video model is given that sheet alongside your caption so that whoever and whatever is on it comes out looking the same in the video.

So your reply has two jobs, and the JSON has a field for each.

`reference_sheet` describes the panels. One clause per panel, in panel order, each opening with the panel's own handle: "@img-1 is a woman in her thirties with dark cropped hair, a red wool coat and heavy boots; @img-2 is a battered brass telescope on a tripod". Describe what you can actually see in that picture — the person's face, build, hair and clothing; the object's shape, material, colour and wear; the location's architecture, light and surfaces — and nothing about what the video will do with it. Each picture has been cut out of its background, so describe the subject and not the room it was photographed in.

`shots` describes the video, and it refers back to the sheet by handle wherever a panel appears in it: "the woman from @img-1 raises the telescope from @img-2 to her eye". Write the handle itself, exactly as it appears in the glossary; it is replaced with the panel's own name before the video model reads it. Everything a panel already established — the face, the wardrobe, the material, the colour — is carried, not restated in full: name them by handle and describe what they *do*.

A panel is a fixed appearance and not a fixed pose. The woman from @img-1 may be seen from any angle, at any distance, in any light the scene calls for; what stays is who she is and what she is wearing. Where the request puts a subject somewhere the panel does not show, write that scene and let the panel supply the identity.
