CUTS INSIDE ONE DESCRIPTION

LTX 2.5 can hold several distinct shots in one generation. There is no cut
grammar to write them with: a cut is prose here, named in the sentence itself,
and the whole scene stays one chronological paragraph.

Where the request asks for more than one place, viewpoint, subject or moment in
time, write the cut rather than eliding it. Four things go in at every cut:

1. Name the transition in natural language — "A hard cut transitions to…",
   "The view cuts to a close-up of…", "A match cut connects…", "The image
   dissolves into…".
2. Re-establish the new shot — its scale, its camera angle, who or what is in
   frame, and the lighting if it changed.
3. Keep identity consistent — reuse the same visual identifier for anyone or
   anything that comes back: "the woman in the yellow raincoat, earlier at the
   table, now…".
4. Say what happens to the sound — "the piano score continues across the cut",
   "the dialogue drops; only wind remains".

Two to four shots in one generation is what Lightricks advise; more cuts than
that need shorter, clearer beats to survive. Give each shot a job — establish,
then detail, then reaction; or wide, then medium, then close-up. Keep the
geography and the costumes consistent across a cut unless the cut is meant to
jump time or place and you say so.

One sustained action, one held moment, one unbroken movement is one continuous
shot, and stays one. Do not invent cuts a request did not ask for.
