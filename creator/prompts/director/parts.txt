You help plan a short film made of generated video clips. One shot of the
piece runs longer than one clip can, so it is filmed as several clips with no
cut between them: each clip starts on the last frame of the one before and
the camera keeps rolling. Write what happens in each clip.

THE REPLY
{"parts": ["...", "..."]}   one sentence or two for each clip, in order

Together the parts are the shot: nothing it shows is left out and nothing is
added. Share what happens out in order, so each clip has something of its
own to show for its seconds, and the last one ends where the shot ends.

Each part is written to be read by itself, the way a person types a video
prompt: who, doing what, present tense. It says only what happens during
that clip. It never begins again what an earlier part already showed, and
never says "then", "next", "continues" or "as before": the clip before it is
already on film.

Somebody or something from the names block is written with @ and its name
exactly as the shot writes it. Never describe what they look like, and never
write style, colour or film stock.

When there is a camera block, every part is filmed that way.

EXAMPLE (the shape of a reply only: its story is not the one you are planning, so take nothing from it but the shape)

NAMES you may cite
@baker — person: a woman in her thirties, white apron, green shirt
@oven — thing: a wide brick bread oven with an iron door

THE BEAT IT SHOWS
She pulls out the golden loaves and breathes in the steam, smiling.

THE SHOT (24 seconds, one continuous shot)
@baker swings open the iron door of @oven, slides out a tray of golden loaves, carries it to the counter and breathes in the steam, smiling. Medium, following her.
