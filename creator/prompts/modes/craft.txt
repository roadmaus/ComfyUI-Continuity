HOW THE PROSE IS WRITTEN
These are the conventions H3 was trained on, distilled from MiniMax's own guide. Your prose uses them; the field names, shot markers and cut times around it are assembled afterwards.

Style opening. The first shot's body opens by naming the overall visual style and the initial composition in the same breath as the scene: "Live-action, cinematic, a medium-wide shot frames a baker opening the shutters of a small street bakery before sunrise." Common styles: cinematic, live-action, 2D-animated, 3D CG, claymation, watercolor, vintage film. Every later shot stays in the style the first one established.

Camera. Write camera movement as a natural English action inside the shot, never as labels stacked at the end: motion type, then amplitude and speed only when they matter. Motion types: Zoom In / Zoom Out, Push In / Pull Out, Pan Left / Pan Right, Truck Left / Truck Right, Tilt Up / Tilt Down, Pedestal Up / Pedestal Down, Arc Shot, Tracking Shot, Static Shot, Shake Slightly / Shake Strongly, POV, Roll Clockwise / Roll Counterclockwise. Amplitude: "with small amplitude" or "with large amplitude". Speed: "at slow speed" or "at fast speed". So: "The camera pushes in with small amplitude at slow speed toward the folded letter in her hands." A later shot's body may open with "the camera cuts to ..." — the cut's written time is added for you. Cross-dissolves, fades and wipes only when the request asks for one.

Speech. A subject who speaks, sings or narrates gets a stable ID — (S1), (S2), (S1,S2) for together — kept identical across every shot; silent characters get none. On a speaker's first appearance, establish the voice outside the tag: age, gender, character type, pitch, timbre, pace or accent. Inside the tag goes only the language and the words: The young woman with a quiet, breathy voice (S1) says: <d>[English] I get off at the next station.</d> For voiceover use exactly "says in an off-screen voiceover" and, straight after the <d> block, state that the on-screen character's lips remain completely closed. A line crossing a cut takes <scenetrans> at both connection points plus a phrase like "continues seamlessly across the cut"; speech truncated by the end of the video takes <cutoff>.

On-screen text. Any visible sign, banner, label or subtitle goes in English double quotation marks, verbatim and untranslated: A red neon sign reading "营业中" glows above the doorway.

overall_soundscape. One paragraph of one to four sentences covering the whole video's ambience, physical action sounds and non-verbal human sounds — wind, traffic, footsteps, fabric, impacts, breathing, laughter — written as heard events: "Steady rain taps against the café windows while low room ambience continues underneath. The entrance bell rings once, followed by wet footsteps and the soft scrape of a chair." Dialogue, singing and diegetic music stay in the shot bodies.

non_diegetic_music. One to three sentences of instrumentation, tempo, rhythm and dynamic change — never mood words or what the music means: "Sparse piano notes at a slow tempo, joined by sustained low strings that gradually increase in volume before fading out."
